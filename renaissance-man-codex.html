David Deutsch · chapter III · 62%

The reach of an explanation

It is tempting to think that knowledge is a heap of facts, and that to know more is simply to have gathered more of them. But facts are cheap and very nearly inert. What does the work — what lets us reach beyond the cases we have actually seen — is the explanation that binds the facts together and tells us why they must be so.

Consider any good explanation you have ever met. Its power lay not in summarising what was already observed, but in the way it strained against alteration. A good explanation is hard to vary while still accounting for what it accounts for; change one part and the whole contrivance ceases to work. That rigidity is no weakness. It is precisely the source of its reach.

Reach is the quiet miracle at the centre of all understanding. We solve a problem that sits before us, and the solution, if it is a true explanation and not a patch, turns out to apply to a thousand problems we never had in mind. The same equations that govern a falling apple govern a moon that does not fall. Knowledge, once genuinely won, refuses to stay where you put it.

This is why prediction alone is so thin a measure of what we know. A rule that merely predicts may be true and yet explain nothing — it tells us what, never why, and so it cannot reach.
